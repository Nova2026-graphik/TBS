It is the first question on every call: "there will be about three hundred of
us, what do I need?" The answer comes down to a handful of ratios, learned in
the field rather than from a catalogue. Here they are, with the reasoning
behind them.

::calculateur-materiel
::

## Seating: always 5 % more

One seat per guest, plus five per cent. This is not sales caution: there are
always unannounced companions, a child who grows up between the invitation and
the day, a driver you sit down. An empty chair costs almost nothing; a guest
left standing through the speeches remembers the reception for that reason.

For a cocktail reception the rule changes entirely: **seating for a third of
the guests is enough**. Nobody sits down at the same time, and filling the room
with chairs kills the circulation that makes the format worthwhile.

## Tables: ten settings, rarely twelve

The round table of ten is the standard in Togo, and for good reason: at twelve,
guests across from one another stop talking and service struggles to get
through. Divide your guest count by ten and round up.

Add **one tablecloth per table, plus one for every sixty guests**: buffets, the
head table and the gift table use them up without anyone thinking of it at
order time.

## Tableware: two and a half plates per diner

For a served dinner — starter, main, shared dessert — count **2.5 plates and
3 pieces of cutlery per diner**. The half comes from overlapping courses: you
never clear fast enough to reuse a plate within the same evening.

Always add **10 % for breakage**. This is not a seller's margin: out of six
hundred pieces of cutlery, a dozen or so do not come back intact, and that is
normal.

## Glassware: the most underestimated item

Two and a half glasses per guest for a reception that ends after dessert.
**Three and a half if the evening runs on** — a guest changes glass when
changing drink, and nobody finds their own again after the first dance.

This is the item most often underestimated in requests, and the one that
triggers the most urgent same-day calls.

## Floor area: 1.3 m² per seated guest

Under a marquee or indoors, count **1.3 m² per seated guest**, circulation and
service included. For a cocktail reception, 0.9 m² is enough.

That figure includes service aisles: a room calculated to the last centimetre
forces waiting staff to squeeze behind guests' backs, and the meal suffers from
it more than from any detail of the decoration.

## Sound: two output points beyond 500

Below one hundred and fifty guests, the room's ambient sound system is often
enough. Between 150 and 500, one system matched to the room. Beyond that,
**two output points beat one more powerful system**: the sound carries without
overwhelming the front tables.

## What these figures do not tell you

They give an order of magnitude, not a floor plan. The venue, the shape of the
room, the running order, a children's area or a caterer's corner all change the
layout — sometimes a great deal.
