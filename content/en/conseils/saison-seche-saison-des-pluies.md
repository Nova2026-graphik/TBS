In Togo the season is not something you choose: a wedding date is set by family
constraints, not meteorological ones. What you put up outdoors, on the other
hand, can be decided with full knowledge of the facts. Here is what each period
actually demands.

## November to March: the dry season

This is the high season for receptions, and for good reasons: little rain, mild
evenings, usable outdoor spaces.

**What it demands anyway:**

- **Dust.** The harmattan, from December to February, lays a fine veil over
  everything left outdoors. Table dressing is done as late as possible, and
  cloths go on after the tables are set up, never before.
- **Light.** Night falls quickly and without a long dusk: plan lighting for
  6.30 p.m., even if the reception starts at 5.
- **Availability.** This is when stock goes fastest. Saturdays in December and
  January are booked four to six weeks ahead, against two for the rest of the
  year.

## April to June: the long rainy season

The rain arrives abruptly, often in late afternoon, and lasts an hour or two.

**What it demands:**

- **A marquee, always**, even under a clear morning sky. An hour of rain on a
  fully dressed set-up means the reception is lost.
- **A floor.** Soaked grass becomes impassable in heels and unstable under
  table legs. Decking, or at the very least a technical mat, across the whole
  reception area.
- **Side sheets.** A marquee without sides protects from nothing as soon as the
  rain is driven by wind. Plan for them, even if you never unroll them.
- **A fallback point** for the sound system and the electrics: technical
  equipment does not tolerate what furniture tolerates.

## July to August: the short dry season

A relative lull, cooler and often overcast. It is the most comfortable window
for an outdoor cocktail: little rain, less heat, no dust.

The one point to watch is ambient humidity, which marks linen and chair covers
if they spend the night outside. Take-down happens the next morning, not the
day after that.

## September to October: the short rainy season

Less water than in June, but continuous humidity.

**What it demands:**

- **Dry storage** for anything made of fabric. A tablecloth put away damp comes
  out stained, and the stain does not come off.
- **Vigilance on outdoor electrics**: extension leads raised off the ground,
  protected connectors, the board under cover.
- **Drying the equipment** before it returns to stock — that is our business,
  but it explains why a take-down in the rain takes longer.

## Three rules that hold all year

**A marquee is not a weather expense, it is insurance.** Its cost against the
total budget of a reception is small; the cost of a ruined set-up cannot be
recovered.

**The ground matters as much as the roof.** A marquee on land that does not
drain protects from the rain that falls, not from the water that rises.

**Plan the electrics twice.** The mains, and a generator. A power cut during
the speeches is not a technical incident in the guests' eyes, it is the memory
of the evening.
